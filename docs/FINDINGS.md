## Challenges that remain

**Telephone audio is imperfect.** VAD threshold and noise reduction reduce false turns but are not acoustic echo cancellation. Speakerphone echo, background speech, accents, carrier transcoding and latency need real-route evaluation. Interruption playback position is estimated from elapsed time, not an exact sample acknowledgement.

**A model is still deciding what to say.** Prompts, constrained tools and correlated approvals prevent many errors but cannot formally guarantee all speech, identity pronunciation or assertions of success. The outcome is the model's structured report of the conversation, not an independently verified booking record.

**Provider failures can be ambiguous.** A lost dial response does not prove that no call exists. Late signed callbacks can recover the control ID; otherwise the carrier-side time limit and operator portal are the fallback. A pending hangup is kept pending instead of declaring success on an arbitrary provider error.

**Private single-instance deployment is deliberate.** In-memory sockets/timers and durable local SQLite keep the architecture simple. Public exposure and horizontal scaling require separate access and state decisions. No authentication, billing or multi-user behavior was added in this review.

**Operational breadth is limited.** Backups and health checks exist, but no automatic backup schedule, retention/deletion UI, history pagination, tracing or alerts are provided. Call history polling returns full records and should be revisited before long-running/high-volume use.

**Dependencies and providers change.** The audit was clean at review time; this is a dated result. Pin the lockfile, re-run CI/audits, and validate provider/model changes with controlled calls.
